# TikTok Shop Affiliate Strategy

Researched September 2026. TikTok changes Shop rules often, so check every number here in the TikTok Shop Affiliate Center before relying on it. Most figures below come from third-party industry blogs, because TikTok's and the FTC's own pages couldn't be fetched from the research environment. They're listed under Sources.

---

## 1. Summary

- **The plan as first described (AI-made "review" videos of products nobody has used) is the version most likely to fail.** It's illegal under the FTC's fake-review rule, TikTok labels and suppresses it, and it converts worse than real footage. Section 3 covers each problem.
- **The version that works is AI-assisted and human-proven.** AI handles research, product selection, scripts, hooks, captions and editing. The proof is real: your hands, the real product, and your actual results. That split lets one person post 1–3 videos a day without cutting corners.
- **Nobody earns commission before 1,000 followers.** Phase 1 builds an audience in one niche using products you buy yourself.
- **Realistic income for a new creator in the first 90 days is about $100–$500 a month**, not thousands. The money comes from the months after that, if you're consistent.

## 3. Why AI-generated reviews backfire

### Legal
The **FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials** took effect **October 21, 2024**. It bans reviews and testimonials that misrepresent:
- that the reviewer exists (AI-generated reviewers are named explicitly), or
- that the reviewer **had actual experience with the product**.

Civil penalties are reported at **about $53,000 per violation**, and each video can count separately. The same rule bans **buying fake followers or views**, so buying followers to speed up growth is also out.

Disclosure is required too. If you get commission or a free sample, that's a "material connection," and you have to say so clearly.

### Platform
- TikTok requires an **AI-generated content (AIGC) label** on realistic AI visuals and audio. It detects unlabeled AI through **C2PA content credentials**, even if you don't self-disclose.
- TikTok Shop reportedly **bans AI that fabricates or replaces product displays** in ways that could mislead buyers.
- Labeled AI content reportedly gets **less distribution**. The Creator Rewards Program reportedly excludes AI content, and AI voices are reportedly banned from promotional LIVEs.
- Your account's **creator quality score** (visible since 2026) affects which products and samples you can access. Policy strikes reduce it.

### Performance
TikTok Shop buyers purchase on **proof**: a stain actually lifting, a drawer actually fitting, a real hand holding a real product. A synthetic demo gives away the one thing that makes these videos sell. Honest "who this is NOT for" videos convert especially well because they're believable.

### How to use AI within the rules

| Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| AI writes the script from **your** test notes | AI makes up an experience you didn't have |
| AI suggests hooks, captions and hashtags | AI avatar presented as a real reviewer |
| AI auto-captions and edits (CapCut) | AI-generated footage of the product "working" |
| AI voiceover **labeled** as AI, over **real** footage | Unlabeled AI voice claiming "I've used this for months" |
| AI analyzes your stats and picks the next product | Buying followers or views |

| Stage | Requirement | What you can do |

**Why this matters:** the free-sample route is mostly closed until you've made sales. In phase 1 you'll **buy 3–5 cheap products yourself**, roughly $60–$120 in total. Niche consistency across your **last 30 videos** reportedly affects your quality score, so pick one niche and stay in it.

## 5. Earnings math

Figures from industry sources:
- Average US affiliate commission: **about 13%**. Beauty, supplements and home: **18–20%**. Electronics and apparel: **10–15%**.
- Open Collaboration (anyone can join): **10–15%**. Targeted Collaboration (by invite): **18%+, negotiable**.
- Video conversion (click to purchase): **3–6%**. LIVE: **5–12%**.
- Products **under $30 convert above 5%**. **Over $80 drops below 1%.**
- New creators (under 10K followers, first 90 days): **about $100–$500 a month**.

**Worked example.** The view-to-click rate is my assumption, not sourced data.

```
Target:          $500/month
Product:         $20 price x 15% commission = $3.00 per sale
Sales needed:    500 / 3              ≈ 167 sales/month
Clicks needed:   167 / 4% conversion  ≈ 4,200 product clicks
Views needed:    4,200 / 1% tap rate  ≈ 420,000 views/month
                 ≈ 14,000 views/day ≈ 7,000 avg views across 2 videos/day
```

Brand-new accounts often get a few hundred views per video. Getting to about 7K average views per video is phase 1 and phase 2. This is why **price band and commission rate matter more than virality**: moving from $3 to $6 per sale halves the views you need.

**Other costs:**
- **Returns claw back commission.** A cheap product with a 3.8★ rating can cost you money. The scorer filters these out.
- **This is 1099 income.** Set aside roughly 25–30% for federal, state and self-employment tax.

## 6. Recommended niche

**Home, kitchen and cleaning problem-solvers under $30.**

| Factor | Home/Kitchen/Cleaning | Beauty (the largest category) |
|---|---|---|
| Share of sales | Large and steady (#CleanTok and #Restock stay popular) | About 40%+ of GMV |
| Commission | 18–20% typical | 18–20% typical |
| Works without showing your face | **Yes.** Hands-only demos are the native format. | Hard, since results show on skin or a face |
| Saturation | Moderate | **Extreme.** Top sellers are dominated by large creators. |
| Proof on camera in 3 seconds | Stains, clutter, before/after | Takes days or weeks to show |
| Cost of samples | Cheap | Cheap, but skin reactions are a liability |

Beauty has the most money in it, but a faceless new account has little advantage there. The home category rewards exactly what you can produce: fast, visual, hands-only proof.

**Alternatives if they suit you better:**
- **Pet products.** Good emotional engagement, if you have a pet.
- **Desk and phone accessories.** Quick demos, but lower commission.
- **Beauty.** Only if you're comfortable on camera and have real skin results to show.

**Pick the niche you'll still enjoy filming at video number 90.** Consistency beats a slightly better category.

## 7. Choosing products

Run candidates through `product_scorer.py` (usage is in `README.md`). It uses these rules:

1. **Price $10–$30 is ideal. Go up to about $50 only if the commission per sale is high.** Conversion falls sharply above $30 and drops below 1% past $80, where the scorer rejects products.
2. **Commission of $2.50 or more per sale is the target.** Below $1.50 the scorer rejects the product, because you'd need too many views.
3. **Store rating of 4.5★ or higher, with 500+ reviews, is the target.** The scorer rejects anything below 4.3★ or with fewer than 100 reviews, which protects you from clawbacks and bad-faith sellers.
4. **The benefit shows on camera in under 3 seconds, without a face.**
5. **It fixes a specific, visible annoyance.** "Makes cleaning easier" is too vague. "Gets the gunk out of the stove gap" works.
6. **Room for a new creator.** Check the product's video tab. If the top 20 videos all have more than 1M views from large accounts, pass.
7. **You'd actually recommend it after testing.** If a product fails your test, that failure is content too ("I tried the viral X so you don't have to").

**Where to find candidates:** the TikTok Shop Affiliate Center (sort by commission and sales), the Kalodata or FastMoss product rankings (both offer free tiers), and the products appearing in your own For You feed after a week of watching only your niche.

## 8. Video formats

Full templates are in `SCRIPTS.md`. Ranked by fit for a new faceless account:

1. **Problem, then satisfying fix** (hands-only)
2. **Honest review: who it's NOT for** (builds trust and converts well)
3. **Side-by-side test** (with vs. without, or cheap vs. expensive)
4. **Stress or durability test**
5. **"Things I'd buy again under $25"** (only products you've actually tested)
6. **Reply to a comment** (turns questions into videos)

**Structure:** hook in 0–2 seconds (show the problem first, not the product), proof from about 2–15 seconds, objection or caveat from about 15–25 seconds, then the call to action. Aim for 15–35 seconds. Add big on-screen text, since most people watch on mute.

**Tools:** use **CapCut**, TikTok's own editor, for auto-captions, templates and trending audio. Custom video software isn't worth building; CapCut already does this well and for free.

## 9. 90-day plan

### Days 1–30: build to 1,000 followers (no commission)
- [ ] Create the account. Use a niche name (e.g. "@fixedit.home"), a one-line bio that says what you test, and a profile photo.
- [ ] **Week 1: don't post.** Spend 20–30 minutes a day watching, liking and saving only niche content, so the algorithm learns what your account is about.
- [ ] Buy 3–5 products that pass the scorer ($60–$120). Test each one for real and write notes (use the template in `SCRIPTS.md`).
- [ ] Post **1–2 videos a day** starting week 2. Film several per product: the demo, the honest review, the test, the reply to a comment.
- [ ] Reply to every comment in the first hour after posting.
- [ ] Don't buy followers or join engagement pods.

### Days 31–60: enter the affiliate pilot (1,000+ followers)
- [ ] Apply in the TikTok Shop Affiliate Center once you reach 1,000.
- [ ] Add product links to your **best-performing** existing formats. Stay under 3 shoppable videos a day.
- [ ] Request samples **only** for products that pass the scorer.
- [ ] Keep about 1 non-shoppable video for every 2 shoppable ones, so the feed doesn't read as all ads.
- [ ] Try one LIVE a week (it converts better). Real voice only.

### Days 61–90: double down on what works
- [ ] Review weekly: which *format* and which *price band* earn the most per 1,000 views?
- [ ] Remake your top 3 videos with new hooks. Re-hooking a proven video often beats starting a new idea.
- [ ] Once you have sales history, pitch sellers directly for Targeted Collaboration rates (18%+).
- [ ] Graduate from the pilot at 5,000 followers plus 30 days.
- [ ] Cross-post the same clips to YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels, so you're not depending on a single platform.

## 10. Checklist before every post

- [ ] **I physically used this product,** and every claim in the video is something I actually observed.
- [ ] Every result shown is **real footage** of this product. No AI-generated product shots.
- [ ] If there's an AI voiceover or AI visuals, **the AI-generated content label is on.**
- [ ] If there's commission or a free sample, **the disclosure is spoken or on screen** ("I earn commission on this," "#ad," "#TikTokShopPartner"), and TikTok's content disclosure toggle is on.
- [ ] No health or medical claims (e.g. "cures," "treats") and no before/after claims I can't back up.
- [ ] The price and deal on screen match the live listing.
- [ ] The product link goes to the product actually shown.

## 12. Decisions (settled September 2026)

1. **Hands only.** Every video opens on the problem, not a face. Use your own voice for voiceovers: an AI voice needs the AI label and reportedly gets less reach.
2. **Budget: $100.** About $75–$80 goes to 4 products and about $20 to an overhead phone mount, since top-down shots are the standard hands-only angle. A stack of books works until the mount arrives.
3. **Niche: home and cleaning.** Start with cheap cleaning tools that show results fast. Skip vacuums, pressure washers and floor machines, which cost over $80, convert poorly and would use up the whole budget on one product.
4. **Time.** The plan assumes about 1–1.5 hours a day (testing, filming 2–3 videos, replying to comments).

### Phase 1 products

| # | Product | Target price | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cordless electric spin scrubber (multiple heads) | $20–$35 | Main earner: highest commission per sale, most filming options |
| 2 | Drain hair clog remover | $10–$20 | Strongest hook (gross, then satisfying) |
| 3 | 3-in-1 spray squeegee / glass wiper | $10–$20 | Streaky to clear on camera. Covers mirrors, shower doors and car windows. |
| 4 | Crevice / gap cleaning brush set | $4–$10 | Growth product, not a money product: under $1 commission per sale, but great hooks |

**Before buying each one, check the listing:**
- 4.5★ or higher, 1,000+ sold, and within the target price
- Read three 1-star reviews. If they describe the same failure, choose a different listing.

The spin scrubber category gets mixed durability reviews (see Consumer Reports and Kimola). Film a follow-up at 30 days whatever happens.
